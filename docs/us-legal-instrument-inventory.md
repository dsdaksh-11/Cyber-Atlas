## Detailed Enactment Dossiers

### 1. Computer Fraud and Abuse Act (CFAA)
- **Official Citation:** 18 U.S.C. § 1030 (Pub. L. 99-474)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 1986
- **Issuing Authority:** United States Department of Justice (DOJ)
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section1030`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **§ 1030(a)(2):** Penalizes intentional unauthorized access to obtain protected financial, governmental, or commercial computer records. Fines and up to 5 years imprisonment (10 years for repeat offenses).
  - **§ 1030(a)(5):** Penalizes transmission of unauthorized code causing impairment or damage to protected computers. Imprisonment up to 10–20 years if critical systems or public health are affected.
  - **§ 1030(g):** Private right of action permitting compensatory damages and injunctive relief where loss exceeds $5,000 in a 1-year period.

### 2. Stored Communications Act (ECPA Title II)
- **Official Citation:** 18 U.S.C. §§ 2701–2712 (Pub. L. 99-508)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 1986
- **Issuing Authority:** United States Department of Justice (DOJ)
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title18-section2701`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **§ 2701:** Criminalizes intentional unauthorized access to a facility through which an electronic communication service is provided, obtaining or altering wire/electronic storage. Fines and up to 5 years imprisonment.
  - **§ 2702:** Restricts voluntary disclosure of customer communications by electronic communications service providers without subscriber consent or court authorization.
  - **§ 2703:** Prescribes statutory procedures (warrants, 2703(d) orders, subpoenas) required for law enforcement access to stored communications.

### 3. HIPAA Security Rule
- **Official Citation:** 45 C.F.R. Part 164, Subpart C (Pub. L. 104-191)
- **Scope:** `SECTORAL` | **Typology:** `REGULATION` | **Year:** 2003
- **Issuing Authority:** HHS Office for Civil Rights (OCR)
- **Direct Link:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-45/subtitle-A/subchapter-C/part-164/subpart-C`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **45 C.F.R. § 164.308:** Administrative Safeguards: Mandatory risk management, workforce security clearances, contingency planning, and business associate contracts.
  - **45 C.F.R. § 164.312:** Technical Safeguards: Unique user identification, emergency access procedures, automatic logoff, and encryption of electronic protected health information (ePHI).
  - **45 C.F.R. § 164.404:** Breach Notification Mandate: Covered entities must notify affected individuals and HHS within **60 calendar days** of discovering a breach of unsecured ePHI.

### 4. Cybersecurity Information Sharing Act of 2015 (CISA)
- **Official Citation:** 6 U.S.C. §§ 1501–1510 (Pub. L. 114-113)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 2015
- **Issuing Authority:** Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA)
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title6/chapter6&edition=prelim`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **6 U.S.C. § 1503:** Authorizes private entities to monitor their information systems and defensively share cyber threat indicators (CTIs).
  - **6 U.S.C. § 1504:** Statutorily protects private entities from civil liability when sharing threat indicators with CISA via the Automated Indicator Sharing (AIS) program.
  - **6 U.S.C. § 1505:** Grants antitrust immunity for private organizations collaborating to detect and mitigate cybersecurity threats.

### 5. Cyber Incident Reporting for Critical Infrastructure Act of 2022 (CIRCIA)
- **Official Citation:** 6 U.S.C. §§ 681–681g (Pub. L. 117-103, Div. Y)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 2022
- **Issuing Authority:** CISA / Department of Homeland Security (DHS)
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?path=/prelim@title6/chapter1/subchapterXXII/partB&edition=prelim`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **6 U.S.C. § 681b(a)(1):** Mandatory **72-hour cyber incident reporting** to CISA by covered critical infrastructure entities following reasonable belief of a substantial cyber incident.
  - **6 U.S.C. § 681b(a)(2):** Mandatory **24-hour ransom payment reporting** to CISA following payment of any extortion demand.
  - **6 U.S.C. § 681c:** CISA administrative subpoena powers to enforce incident disclosures against non-responsive entities.

### 6. Electronic Signatures in Global and National Commerce Act (E-SIGN Act)
- **Official Citation:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 7001–7031 (Pub. L. 106-229)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 2000
- **Issuing Authority:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC) / SEC
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title15-section7001`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **15 U.S.C. § 7001(a):** Establishes that a signature, contract, or record may not be denied legal effect solely because it is in electronic format.
  - **15 U.S.C. § 7001(c):** Requires affirmative consumer consent prior to substituting paper notices with electronic records.

### 7. Gramm-Leach-Bliley Act Safeguards Rule (GLBA Safeguards)
- **Official Citation:** 16 C.F.R. Part 314 (under 15 U.S.C. §§ 6801, 6805)
- **Scope:** `SECTORAL` | **Typology:** `REGULATION` | **Year:** 2002 (Amended 2023)
- **Issuing Authority:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
- **Direct Link:** `https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-16/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-314`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **16 C.F.R. § 314.3:** Financial institutions must maintain a comprehensive written information security program including multi-factor authentication (MFA) and data encryption.
  - **16 C.F.R. § 314.4(h):** Mandatory notification to the FTC within **30 days** of discovering a security breach involving at least 500 consumers.
  - **15 U.S.C. § 45:** Non-compliance prosecuted under FTC Act Section 5 with civil penalties up to $50,120 per violation.

### 8. Federal Rules of Evidence (FRE Rules 902(13) & 902(14))
- **Official Citation:** Fed. R. Evid. 902(13), 902(14)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `CODE_PROVISION` | **Year:** 2017
- **Issuing Authority:** Supreme Court of the United States
- **Direct Link:** `https://www.rulesofevidence.org/article-ix/rule-902/`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **Rule 902(13):** Machine-generated electronic records are self-authenticating upon written certification by a qualified person, eliminating the requirement of live witness foundation.
  - **Rule 902(14):** Certified digital forensic copies verified by cryptographic hash values (MD5/SHA-256) are self-authenticating.

### 9. Children's Online Privacy Protection Act of 1998 (COPPA)
- **Official Citation:** 15 U.S.C. §§ 6501–6506 (Pub. L. 105-277; 16 C.F.R. Part 312)
- **Scope:** `FEDERAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 1998
- **Issuing Authority:** Federal Trade Commission (FTC)
- **Direct Link:** `https://uscode.house.gov/view.xhtml?req=granuleid:USC-prelim-title15-section6501`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **15 U.S.C. § 6502(b)(1)(A):** Operators of websites directed to children under 13 must obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information.
  - **16 C.F.R. § 312.8:** Mandatory reasonable security procedures to protect children's online personal data.
  - **15 U.S.C. § 6505:** Violations enforceable as unfair trade practices with fines exceeding $50,000 per violation.

### 10. Post-Wayfair Economic Nexus & Marketplace Facilitator Acts
- **Official Citation:** SSUTA / State Codified Economic Nexus Standards (585 U.S. 500)
- **Scope:** `STATE_PROVINCIAL` | **Typology:** `ACT` | **Year:** 2018
- **Issuing Authority:** Multistate Tax Commission (MTC) / Streamlined Sales Tax Board
- **Direct Link:** `https://www.streamlinedsalestax.org/`
- **Key Provisions:**
  - **Economic Nexus Standard:** Remote sellers exceeding $100,000 in gross electronic sales or 200 separate transactions within a state are required to collect state sales tax.
  - **Marketplace Facilitator Duty:** Major electronic commerce platforms are legally deemed the seller responsible for calculating and remitting sales taxes on third-party marketplace transactions.
