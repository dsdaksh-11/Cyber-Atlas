## 3. Detailed Jurisdictional Analyses & Patterns

### A. The Comprehensive Statutory Model: India (`IN`)
India serves as the first fully completed jurisdiction in CyberLaw Atlas:
- **15 verified statutory instruments** covering all 9 project areas.
- **Evidence standard met:** All 15 instruments link to primary government gazettes (*Gazette of India*, *India Code*, *RBI Master Directions*, *MeitY*, *CERT-In*).
- **Modernized framework:** Captures the July 1, 2024 transition to the *Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS)*, *Bharatiya Sakshya Adhiniyam (BSA)*, and *Bharatiya Nagarik Suraksha Sanhita (BNSS)*, establishing specialized digital evidence certificate requirements under Section 63 BSA.

### B. The Fragmented Federal Model: United States (`US`)
The United States represents a distinct constitutional pattern:
- The US does not possess a single omnibus data protection act or unified national cybersecurity code.
- Instead, cyber governance is divided across:
  - Federal criminal statutes (*CFAA 18 U.S.C. § 1030*)
  - Surveillance and communications privacy (*ECPA 18 U.S.C. §§ 2510-2523, 2701-2713*)
  - Federal cybersecurity information sharing (*CISA 6 U.S.C. §§ 1501-1510*)
  - Sector-specific statutes (HIPAA for healthcare, GLBA for financial institutions, COPPA for children's online privacy, FERPA for education)
  - State omnibus legislation (California CCPA/CPRA, Virginia VCDPA, Colorado CPA)
- **Status:** Currently documented with 3 federal baseline acts. Full expansion of sector-specific federal statutes is prioritized in Priority 1 of the research backlog.

### C. The Integrated Penal Code Model: Continental Europe
Many European jurisdictions (Germany, France, Spain, Sweden, Norway, Denmark, Switzerland, Austria, Belgium, Poland, Portugal) integrate cyber offenses directly into their general criminal codes:
- Cybercrime is codified as specific chapters within the *Strafgesetzbuch*, *Code Pénal*, or *Código Penal*.
- General data protection is harmonized under the EU GDPR (Regulation (EU) 2016/679), supplemented by national execution statutes.
- **Status:** The platform distinguishes these as `CODE_PROVISION` instruments rather than standalone acts, avoiding false inflation of law counts.

### D. The Dual Core Law Model: Developing & Middle-Income Economies
Many jurisdictions in Africa, Latin America, and Southeast Asia (e.g., Nigeria, Kenya, Ghana, Morocco, Peru, Colombia, Chile, Philippines) established cyber governance through two cornerstone enactments:
1. A dedicated Cybercrime / Computer Misuse Act
2. A comprehensive Personal Data Protection Act
- **Status:** These countries legitimately have 2–3 primary statutes. Secondary regulations (such as critical infrastructure protection directives and central bank cyber guidelines) are queued for systematic research.

## 4. Remediation and Expansion Roadmap

Following the successful completion of the India completeness test, the remaining 47 jurisdictions will be upgraded in three structured research waves:

1. **Wave 1: Major Common Law & Federated Systems**
   - United States, United Kingdom, Australia, Canada, Singapore.
   - Objective: Expand sector-specific regulations (financial, health, critical infrastructure) and state/provincial statutes.
2. **Wave 2: Major Civil Law & European Union Member States**
   - Germany, France, Netherlands, Spain, Italy, Switzerland, Japan, South Korea, China, Brazil.
   - Objective: Audit national GDPR execution statutes, NIS2 transpositions, and sectoral central bank guidelines.
3. **Wave 3: Emerging Jurisdictions in Middle East, Africa, Latin America & Asia**
   - Remaining 32 jurisdictions.
   - Objective: Primary gazette URL disambiguation, electronic evidence provisions extraction, and consumer protection e-commerce rules.
